# NESTRIS 3D — Technical Specification
## Version 0.2 (Implementation Blueprint)

**Genre:** 3D falling-block puzzle / spatial-density survival  
**Document Scope:** Piece geometry and generation math, coordinate systems, rotation and kick systems, collision, spawn rules, post-lock movement ("Seam Slide"), nesting mechanics, clearing, scoring, difficulty, randomizer, ghost systems, and rendering constraints. All open questions from v0.1 are resolved; all pseudocode is executable in intent.

---

## 1. Overview and Terminology

NESTRIS 3D is played in a vertical rectangular well. Pieces are **mated polycubes**: three-dimensional solids generated by a multiplicative (intersection-product) combination of two classic tetrominoes embedded in perpendicular planes. Pieces spawn in one of three size classes (½×, 1×, 2×). The player survives by densifying the stack — nesting small pieces into the concavities and pockets of larger ones — because 3D geometry wastes volume far faster than 2D Tetris does, and wasted volume is death.

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Unit (u)** | The edge length of one cell of a normal-size (1×) piece. |
| **Subcell** | The engine's true grid resolution: ½ u. All geometry, collision, and clearing operate in subcells. |
| **Well** | The play volume. Default 8 u × 8 u × 16 u = 16 × 16 × 32 subcells (W × D × H). |
| **Parent tetromino** | One of the 7 classic shapes {I, O, T, S, Z, L, J} as a planar 4-cell polyomino. |
| **Mated piece** | The 3D solid produced by the mating product of two parent tetrominoes (§3). |
| **Slab** | One horizontal layer of subcells (16 × 16 = 256 subcells). The clearing granularity. |
| **Pocket** | An empty region enclosed or shielded by a placed piece's geometry; the target volume for nesting (§10). |
| **Seam Slide** | The single-axis post-lock movement grace window (§9). |
| **Host** | A locked piece that owns one or more pockets. |
| **Guest** | A piece that locks inside a host's pocket field and registers a nest. |
| **Channel axis** | The single axis along which a locked piece may Seam Slide (§9.1). |

## 2. Coordinate System, Units, and the Well

Right-handed coordinates. **x** = width (left/right), **y** = depth (toward/away from camera), **z** = height (up). Gravity acts along −z.

All positions and extents are stored as **integer subcell coordinates**. The well occupies:
- x ∈ [0, 16)
- y ∈ [0, 16)
- z ∈ [0, 32)

Two additional hidden slabs above the well (z ∈ [32, 36)) form the **spawn buffer**; a piece locking with any voxel at z ≥ 32 triggers the fail state (§15).

The choice of ½ u resolution means every size class lands on the integer grid:

| Size class | Scale | Cell edge in subcells | A cell's voxel count |
|------------|-------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Half (½×) | 0.5× | 1 | 1 subcell |
| Normal (1×) | 1.0× | 2 | 8 subcells (2×2×2) |
| Double (2×) | 2.0× | 4 | 64 subcells (4×4×4) |

There is deliberately no finer resolution and no rotation off the 90° lattice: every voxel of every piece at every legal orientation and position is axis-aligned and integer-addressed. This keeps collision exact and branch-free (§7).

## 3. Piece Geometry: The Mating Product

### 3.1 Parent embedding
Each parent tetromino is a set of 4 cells in a plane.
- **Parent A** is embedded in the XZ plane: A ⊂ ℤ², cells addressed (x, z).
- **Parent B** is embedded in the YZ plane: B ⊂ ℤ², cells addressed (y, z).

The two planes share the z axis (the vertical). Each parent may be used in any of its planar orientations (up to 4 rotations; mirror is not allowed — S and Z remain distinct, as do L and J).

### 3.2 The product operator ⊗
The mated piece M = A ⊗ B is defined multiplicatively on indicator functions. Writing 𝟙_A(x,z) ∈ {0,1} and 𝟙_B(y,z) ∈ {0,1}:

**𝟙_M(x, y, z) = 𝟙_A(x, z) · 𝟙_B(y, z)**

A voxel exists at (x,y,z) iff its XZ shadow falls inside A and its YZ shadow falls inside B. Equivalently, per horizontal row: if row z of A contains the x-values R_A(z) and row z of B contains the y-values R_B(z), then row z of M is the Cartesian product R_A(z) × R_B(z).

**Volume formula.** With r_A(z) = |R_A(z)| and r_B(z) = |R_B(z)|:

**V(M) = Σ_z r_A(z) · r_B(z)**

Volume ranges from 4 cells (two vertical I's) to 16 cells (two horizontal I's in the same row). This is the literal sense in which the geometry is multiplicative: each height-row of the solid is the arithmetic product of the two silhouettes' row widths.

### 3.3 Vertical alignment offset δ
Parents may occupy different z-extents (a vertical I spans 4 rows; a horizontal I spans 1). Before taking the product, B is shifted vertically by an integer offset δ, and the product is taken over the overlapping rows. Different δ values yield different — sometimes radically different — solids from the same parent pair. **δ is part of the piece's identity**: a generated piece is fully specified by the tuple (A, orient_A, B, orient_B, δ, size).

### 3.4 Validation rules (procedural generation)
The generator enumerates candidate tuples and accepts a piece only if all of the following hold:

1. **Non-degeneracy:** V(M) ≥ 4 cells.
2. **Boundedness:** V(M) ≤ 16 cells and the bounding box fits in 4×4×4 cells.
3. **Connectivity:** the voxel set is face-connected (6-connectivity). Disconnected products are rejected outright — no largest-component salvage, because a salvaged fragment no longer honestly reflects its parents.
4. **Spawnability:** at least one of the piece's 24 orientations fits within the well's horizontal cross-section at its size class. (A 2× piece with a 4-cell-wide footprint is 16 subcells wide — exactly the well width — and is legal but cannot be translated laterally in that orientation; the generator permits it, the difficulty curve controls its frequency.)
5. **Canonical deduplication:** two tuples producing congruent voxel sets (identical up to the 24 rotations) collapse to one canonical piece, so the bag isn't secretly biased toward high-symmetry shapes.

Enumeration over 7 parents × ≤4 orientations each × legal δ values, after deduplication, yields the generated set. A reference implementation produced **48 unique canonical solids** (§20).

### 3.5 Worked examples
Using row notation z: {x-values} ⊗ {y-values}:

**T ⊗ T, both stem-down (the CRADLE).** A = T with stem at bottom: row 0 = {1}, row 1 = {0,1,2}. Same for B. Product: row 0 = 1×1 = 1 cell at (1,1,0); row 1 = 3×3 = 9 cells. V = 10. The result is a 3×3 plate levitating on a single central stem — leaving a ring of 8 empty cells of overhang beneath the plate. This is the archetypal nesting host.

**L ⊗ T (the GNOMON).** A = L: row 0 = {0,1}, row 1 = {0}, row 2 = {0}. B = T stem-up: row 0 = {0,1,2}, row 1 = {1}. δ = 0 product: row 0 = 2×3 = 6 cells; row 1 = 1×1 = 1 cell at (0,1,1). V = 7. A 2×3 slab with one off-center stud.

**S ⊗ Z (the HELIX).** A = S: row 0 = {1,2}, row 1 = {0,1}. B = Z: row 0 = {0,1}, row 1 = {1,2}. Product: two 2×2 plates offset diagonally in opposite senses, joined through the shared (1,1) column. V = 8. A chiral step that creates a wraparound pocket when two HELIXes interlock.

**O ⊗ O (the REACTOR).** 2×2 on both silhouettes over 2 rows: V = 8, a perfect 2×2×2 cube. The only fully convex piece of volume 8; trivially stackable, contributes nothing to nesting.

**I ⊗ I, both vertical (the MONOLITH).** 1×1×4 column, V = 4. The minimum-volume piece and the game's gap-filling scalpel, especially at ½ size.

### 3.6 Legendary pieces
Legendaries are hand-curated tuples with fixed names, guaranteed bag appearances (§14), and distinct emissive colorways. They are mechanically identical to generated pieces — no special rules — but are chosen because their geometry anchors strategy. They are **excluded from the tiered generated pools** to prevent double-dipping.

| Name | Tuple | V | Strategic identity |
|------|-------|---|-------------------|
| **MONOLITH** | I(vert) ⊗ I(vert) | 4 | Precision gap filler |
| **REACTOR** | O ⊗ O | 8 | Convex foundation block |
| **CRADLE** | T(stem-down) ⊗ T(stem-down) | 10 | Premier nesting host: 8-cell pocket ring |
| **HELIX** | S ⊗ Z | 8 | Chiral interlock; pairs with its mirror |
| **PLATE** | I(horiz) ⊗ I(horiz), δ=0 | 16 | Maximum volume; slab finisher and pocket "lid" |

## 4. Size Classes

Every drawn piece is instantiated at one of three scales. Scale multiplies the piece's cell lattice, not just its render size:

| Class | Cell edge | Typical bounding box (subcells) | Bag weight (baseline) |
|-------|-----------|--------------------------------|----------------------|
| Half (½×) | 1 subcell | ≤ 4×4×4 | 25% |
| Normal (1×) | 2 subcells | ≤ 8×8×8 | 55% |
| Double (2×) | 4 subcells | ≤ 16×16×16 | 20% |

**Three consequences the engine must honor:**

1. **Sub-lattice placement.** A ½× piece can occupy odd subcell coordinates — it addresses the grid at twice the spatial frequency of a 1× piece. This is what makes nesting geometrically real: a ½× MONOLITH (4×1×1 subcells) slides into seams no 1× piece can enter.
2. **Fall speed scales inversely with size.** Perceptually, larger masses should feel heavier but fall no faster; mechanically, Half pieces fall at 1.25× the current gravity rate, Doubles at 0.8×, giving the player more decision time on exactly the pieces that can ruin a board.
3. **Doubles create the pockets that Halves fill.** A 2× CRADLE's pocket ring is 8 cells × 64 subcells = 512 subcells of shielded volume — enough to swallow several entire ½× pieces. The size system and the nesting system are one system.

**Position representation.** A piece's position is the world subcell coordinate of its anchor cell (the original (0,0,0) cell of the parent lattice). All other voxels are derived by:
```
voxel = (pos.x + cx*scale, pos.y + cy*scale, pos.z + cz*scale)
```
where (cx, cy, cz) are the integer cell coordinates from the canonical 1× lattice. Rotation tables are computed on the unit lattice, then scaled in-place. This guarantees that ½× pieces sit on odd coordinates when spawned at odd anchors.

## 5. Orientation and Rotation

### 5.1 Orientation space
Pieces rotate on the 90° lattice only. The orientation space is the chiral octahedral rotation group (order 24). Each piece precomputes up to 24 voxel sets at load time (fewer after symmetry collapse — the REACTOR has 1 distinct orientation, the MONOLITH 3). Runtime rotation is a table lookup, never a matrix transform.

Inputs map to quarter-turn generators: **pitch** (about x), **yaw** (about z), **roll** (about y). Orientation state is an index 0–23; each generator is a precomputed permutation of indices.

### 5.2 3D kick table
When a rotation is blocked, the engine attempts translated placements in strict order, at offsets scaled to the piece's size class (offsets below in subcells for a 1× piece; halve for ½×, double for 2×):

```
(0,0,0), (−2,0,0), (+2,0,0), (0,−2,0), (0,+2,0),
(0,0,+2), (−2,−2,0), (+2,+2,0), (−2,+2,0), (+2,−2,0), (0,0,+4)
```

The first collision-free candidate is accepted; if none succeed, the rotation is refused and orientation is unchanged. The two upward kicks (+z) permit T-spin-style tuck maneuvers under overhangs — with CRADLE-class pockets in play, upward kicks are the primary skilled-play verb, so their position late in the table (making them a last resort rather than an accident) is intentional.

**Kick scaling safety:** Since all base offsets are even multiples of 2 subcells, halving for ½× yields integers (−2 → −1, +2 → +1, +4 → +2). No rounding ambiguity exists.

### 5.3 Rotation legality inside pockets
No special-case rules: a rotation inside a pocket is legal iff the destination voxel set is collision-free after kicks. Pocket ceilings naturally forbid most rotations, which is what makes pre-rotation before entering a pocket a skill.

## 6. Spawn System

### 6.1 Spawn origin algorithm
Each canonical piece + orientation + size class precomputes a **spawn offset** (sx, sy, sz) at load time.

**Default spawn:**
- **Horizontal (x, y):** Centered at the well midpoint, rounded down. For a piece with bounding box (bbx, bby, bbz) in subcells at its current size class:
  ```
  sx = floor((16 - bbx) / 2)
  sy = floor((16 - bby) / 2)
  ```
- **Vertical (z):** The piece spawns at the **bottom of the spawn buffer**, i.e., sz = 32 - bbz. This places the piece as high as possible without clipping into the buffer ceiling, giving maximum fall distance and decision time.

**Fallback for wide pieces:** If bbx > 16 or bby > 16 in the current orientation, the piece cannot be centered. In this case:
- If bbx > 16: sx = 0 (flush left)
- If bby > 16: sy = 0 (flush front)
- If both exceed 16: the orientation is **unspawnable** and must not be selected by the randomizer for that size class.

### 6.2 Spawn collision precheck
When the randomizer produces a piece (shape + orientation + size), the engine performs a spawn collision test **before the piece becomes active**:
1. Compute spawn position using the algorithm above.
2. Run the standard collision test (§7.2) at that position.
3. If collision: the game ends immediately (fail state §15, condition a).

**No orientation fallback at spawn.** The randomizer commits to a specific orientation; if that orientation collides at spawn, it is a true lockout. The randomizer must respect spawnability (§3.4, rule 4) to prevent guaranteed death on spawn.

### 6.3 Spawn buffer semantics
The spawn buffer (z ∈ [32, 36)) is not part of the well for clearing or occupancy purposes. It exists solely to:
1. Provide a visible "above the well" region where pieces materialize.
2. Define the fail condition: any voxel at z ≥ 32 at lock time ends the game (§15, condition b).

## 7. Collision and Occupancy

### 7.1 Data structure
The well is a **bitboard**: one bit per subcell, organized as 32 slabs × 4 × uint64 (256 bits per slab), 128 words total. The active piece is stored the same way, as a sparse list of occupied slab-words at its current position.

### 7.2 Tests
- **Broad phase:** integer AABB of the piece vs. well bounds; reject out-of-bounds immediately.
- **Narrow phase:** for each slab the piece intersects, `piece_bits & well_bits ≠ 0` ⇒ collision. Worst case (2× piece, 16-slab extent) is 64 AND operations.

All movement (gravity step, lateral step, rotation candidate, kick candidate, Seam Slide step) reduces to this one test at a candidate position. There is no continuous physics, no floating point, no tunneling.

### 7.3 Lock condition
A piece locks when a gravity step is blocked and the lock delay (500 ms baseline, −15 ms/level, floor 200 ms) elapses without a successful downward move. Successful lateral moves or rotations during lock delay reset it, up to a cap of 8 resets (anti-infinite-stall, per modern Tetris convention). On lock, the piece's bits are OR-ed into the well and the Seam Slide window opens (§9).

---

## 8. Gravity and Fall Timing

Gravity is expressed in subcells per second and follows a per-level curve:

```
g(level) = 2 · 1.28^(level − 1)   subcells/sec   (levels 1–20, then fixed at level 20 rate)
```

Modified by size class:
- Half: ×1.25
- Normal: ×1.0
- Double: ×0.8

Soft drop multiplies current gravity ×20; hard drop is instantaneous with lock delay skipped but the Seam Slide window preserved — hard drop commits your column, not your final seat.

Level advances every 10 slabs cleared.

## 9. Seam Slide (Post-Lock Grace Movement)

### 9.1 Channel axis definition
Every piece has exactly one channel axis, computed once at generation time from its geometry and stored per-orientation in the precomputed tables.

**Algorithm:**
1. A piece is **prismatic** along axis `a` only if:
   - Its extent along `a` is ≥ 2 subcells (at 1× scale), **and**
   - Every cross-section perpendicular to `a` is identical (voxel-for-voxel congruent).
2. If prismatic along one or more axes, the channel axis is the prismatic axis with the **maximum extent**. Tiebreak: x > y > z.
3. If no axis is prismatic, the channel axis is the axis of the **longest bounding-box extent**. Tiebreak: x > y > z.

**Examples:**
- MONOLITH (1×1×4): Prismatic along z (extent 4, max). Channel axis = z. It slides vertically only.
- PLATE (4×4×1): Prismatic along x and y (both extent 4). Tiebreak gives x. Channel axis = x.
- REACTOR (2×2×2): Prismatic along x, y, and z (all extent 2). Tiebreak gives x. Channel axis = x.
- CRADLE (3×3×2 with stem): Not prismatic (cross-sections vary). Longest extent is x or y (3). Tiebreak gives x. Channel axis = x.

The channel axis rotates with the piece. A piece whose channel axis currently points along z can Seam Slide only vertically — usually useless, occasionally a lifesaver under an overhang — which makes final orientation a resource, not just a fit decision.

### 9.2 The window
On lock, a window of **W(level) = max(120, 400 − 10·level)** ms opens. During it:
- The piece may translate in ±1-subcell steps along its channel axis only. No rotation. No other axis.
- Each successful step costs **40 ms** of remaining window (sliding is not free).
- The window ends at expiry or on the player's confirm input, whichever is first.

### 9.3 Settle rule
If Seam Sliding leaves the piece unsupported (no voxel has an occupied subcell or the floor directly beneath it), then at window close the piece drops straight down to the first supported position and hard-locks with no further grace. You can slide off a shelf; the floor will collect you.

### 9.4 Hard drop interaction
When a piece is hard-dropped:
1. It instantly descends to the lowest collision-free position.
2. It locks immediately (lock delay is skipped).
3. The Seam Slide window opens at the drop position with full duration W(level).
4. The hard drop distance is measured from the pre-drop location for scoring purposes (§13).

## 10. Nesting and Pockets

### 10.1 Pocket ownership semantics
To eliminate double-counting and order-dependency when multiple hosts' pocket fields overlap, the engine maintains a **global pocket ownership table**: one host ID (or null) per well subcell. A subcell can belong to at most one host's pocket field at any time.

**Assignment rule:** When a host's pocket field is computed or recomputed, every pocket subcell within its inflated bounding box is written to the global table with that host's ID. If a subcell was previously owned by a different host, the new host overwrites it. The most recently updated host "wins." This is deterministic and cheap.

### 10.2 Pocket detection (host-side)
When a piece locks, the engine computes its pocket field within the piece's bounding box inflated by 1 subcell:

```
For each empty subcell c in Ω (inflated AABB):
  shielded = 0
  For dir in {+x, −x, +y, −y, +z, −z}:
    Walk from c along dir until leaving Ω or hitting a voxel of H
    If hit: shielded += 1
  If shielded ≥ 4: mark c as pocket cell

Pockets = flood_fill(pocket cells)
Discard pockets with volume < 4 subcells (noise)
```

Each pocket stores its volume and its **aperture**: the set of its cells with an unshielded direction (the mouth you enter through).

Pocket fields are recomputed **lazily** for hosts whose inflated bounding box intersects the inflated bounding box of any newly locked piece (§10.4). The recomputed pocket overwrites the global ownership table for its subcells.

### 10.3 Nest registration (guest-side)
When a piece locks, the engine checks its voxels against the **global pocket ownership table**:

```
For each host H whose ID appears in the ownership table for any voxel of guest G:
  inside = count of G's voxels whose ownership table entry == H's ID
  If inside / |voxels(G)| ≥ 0.75:
    Register nest(G → H)   // fires scoring + density event
    Break                    // one host per nest; first match wins
```

Nests are events (they fire scoring and the density system), not ongoing state — the voxels simply merge into the well like any others.

### 10.4 Adjacency trigger for lazy recomputation
A host H is flagged for pocket recomputation when a newly locked piece L satisfies:

```
inflated_AABB(H, 1 subcell) ∩ inflated_AABB(L, 1 subcell) ≠ ∅
```

That is, their bounding boxes inflated by 1 subcell intersect. This is conservative enough to catch sealing/subdivision cases (including lid pieces placed one cell above a pocket) without being so broad as to recompute the entire well on every lock.

### 10.5 Nesting and clearing
Nested voxels participate in slab clears exactly like all voxels. There is no separate "clear the nest" rule; the reward for nesting is (a) the immediate nest bonus (§13), (b) the density contribution (§12), and (c) the plain geometric fact that a filled pocket is a fillable slab. The design principle: nesting is never a special case in the clearing rules — it is a strictly geometric act with economic consequences.

## 11. Slab Clears

A slab clears when all 256 of its subcells are occupied. On clear:
- All voxels in the slab are deleted.
- Everything above descends by whole slabs (sticky column gravity, classic Tetris behavior: the well contents above translate down 1 subcell per cleared slab, as a rigid mass per column).
- A **Cascade variant** (connected-component re-fall) is specced as a selectable mode (§18) but is not the default; cascade physics plus nesting produces chain economies that belong in a variant, not the core game.

Simultaneous multi-slab clears score super-linearly (§13).

Because a 1× piece is 2 subcells tall, the natural clearing rhythm is 2-slab pairs; single-slab clears are the signature of successful ½×-piece surgery, and 4-slab ("QUAD") clears require deliberate Double-piece foundations.

## 12. Pressure and the Density Economy

The stack's density is measured over its occupied envelope:

```
density = (occupied subcells) / (subcells in all slabs at or below the highest occupied slab)
```

Density is the survival currency and closes the loop on the game's premise — 3D stacking wastes volume unless you nest.

To prevent oscillation death spirals and provide tactical clarity, density is computed as a **rolling average over the last 10 piece locks** (including the current lock). This smooths out transient spikes from slab clears.

| Density state | Threshold | Effect |
|-------------|-----------|--------|
| **Breathing** | rolling_avg > 0.75 | Gravity multiplier ×0.9. The stack "breathes"; visual: floor grid pulses cool cyan. |
| **Neutral** | 0.45 ≤ rolling_avg ≤ 0.75 | No modifier. |
| **Pressure** | rolling_avg < 0.45 | Gravity multiplier ×1.15 and the Pressure vignette appears (rising magenta bloom at the well rim). |

The multipliers apply on top of the level curve. A player who stacks 3D pieces the way one stacks 2D tetrominoes will hover near 0.45 density and be accelerated to death; a player who nests will hold 0.75+ and buy time. This is the enforcement mechanism for the core strategy statement: lasting longest requires nesting the small pieces inside the larger ones.

**Lock delay pressure (optional tuning):** In v0.3, consider also applying a ±50 ms lock delay modifier in Breathing/Pressure states to vary tactical feel without further gravity escalation.

## 13. Scoring

| Event | Score |
|-------|-------|
| Soft drop | 1 point per subcell of vertical distance fallen **while soft-drop is held** (not total fall distance) |
| Hard drop | 2 points per subcell of vertical distance from pre-drop position to lock position |
| Single slab clear | 100 × level |
| Double slab | 300 × level |
| Triple slab | 500 × level |
| QUAD (4 slabs) | 800 × level |
| 5+ slabs ("COLLAPSE") | 1200 × level + 200 per extra slab beyond 5 |
| Nest bonus | 4 × (nested subcells) × level |
| Perfect nest (100% of guest voxels pocketed AND pocket ≥ 90% filled) | ×3 the nest bonus |
| Seam Slide save (nest registered on a piece that Seam Slid ≥ 2 steps) | +150 × level |
| All-clear (empty well) | 3000 × level |

**Back-to-back multi-slab clears** carry a ×1.5 chain multiplier, Tetris-standard. A back-to-back is any clear of 2+ slabs immediately following another 2+ slab clear with no intervening non-clearing lock.

**All-clear timing:** Checked immediately after any clear completes and all gravity/settling resolves. If the well contains zero occupied subcells, the all-clear bonus fires. Chain clears that happen to empty the well qualify.

## 14. Randomizer

A dual-bag system with independent shape and size bags.

### 14.1 Shape bag
The generated set (§3.4) is partitioned into difficulty tiers by **convexity deficiency** = V / bounding_box_volume:

| Tier | Threshold | Character |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **Convex** | convexity_ratio ≥ 0.75 | Compact, stackable, low nesting value |
| **Moderate** | 0.50 ≤ convexity_ratio < 0.75 | Balanced geometry |
| **Pathological** | convexity_ratio < 0.50 | Concave, high nesting value, the interesting hosts |

Each bag of 12 draws:
- 5 from Convex
- 4 from Moderate
- 2 from Pathological
- **Exactly 1 Legendary** (drawn from the separate legendary pool: MONOLITH, REACTOR, CRADLE, HELIX, PLATE)

The 12 draws are shuffled. Legendary pieces are **excluded** from the tiered pools.

### 14.2 Size bag
An independent 20-draw bag containing:
- 5 Half
- 11 Normal
- 4 Double

Shuffled. Sizes are assigned to shapes at spawn.

### 14.3 Preview and hold
Preview queue shows the next 4 pieces with sizes, rendered at true relative scale. One hold slot, Tetris-standard rules (no re-hold until a spawn).

**Strategic constraint:** The independence of the two bags means you can see a ½× MONOLITH coming, but you cannot make one come.

## 15. Fail State

The game ends when either:
- **(a)** A spawning piece's initial position collides with the well contents (spawn lockout, §6.2).
- **(b)** A piece locks with any voxel in the spawn buffer (z ≥ 32).

Death animation: the neon edge-glow of every placed piece gutters out slab by slab from the top down — the smothering, rendered.

## 16. Visual Language (Engineering-Relevant Notes)

Sleek minimalist neon; the aesthetic doubles as information design, so these are functional requirements:

- **Material:** pieces render as near-black glass volumes with emissive edges (bloom-post-processed). Fill opacity ~12% so that pockets and nested guests remain visible through hosts — occlusion transparency is not decoration, it is how the player reads nest opportunities.
- **Color channels:** hue encodes parent-pair family (generated pieces), fixed signature hues for legendaries; edge intensity encodes size class (Half = thin crisp lines, Double = heavy corona). Nested guests re-tint to their host's hue at 60% saturation on registration, visually "absorbed."
- **Ghost systems:** standard drop-ghost at the landing position, plus a pocket ghost (§17).
- **Well:** wireframe grid at subcell pitch on the floor, unit pitch on the walls, so the two resolutions of the game are always both on screen.

## 17. Ghost Systems

### 17.1 Standard drop ghost
Rendered at the piece's final collision-free position along −z from its current location. Computed with a single downward ray-cast per frame.

### 17.2 Pocket ghost
**Purpose:** When the active piece's current orientation could register a nest reachable via the remaining Seam Slide budget, the target pocket's aperture edges pulse.

**Computation:**
1. Determine the piece's channel axis in its current orientation.
2. If the piece is not near any pocket (AABB distance to any host's inflated box > W(level) in subcells), skip.
3. Otherwise, perform a bounded tree search along the channel axis:
   - Max depth = floor(W(level) / 40 ms) ≤ 10 steps (at level 1, W=390ms → 9 steps; at level 20+, W=120ms → 3 steps).
   - Branching factor = 2 (±1 subcell along channel axis).
   - At each node, test collision at that position.
   - At leaf nodes, test the 75% nest condition against the global pocket ownership table.
4. If any leaf satisfies the nest condition, pulse the aperture edges of the target pocket.

**Performance budget:** Worst-case ~2^10 = 1024 collision tests per frame, but early termination (collision, no nearby hosts) reduces this to <100 in practice. The search runs only when the active piece is within 10 subcells of its lock height. This is entirely feasible at 60 Hz.

## 18. Variants

### 18.1 Cascade gravity (v0.3 prototype)
A selectable mode where disconnected components above a cleared slab re-fall independently rather than descending as rigid columns.

**Design note:** Cascade physics interacts with nest registration. A filled pocket that clears its host slab might leave the nest guest now unsupported and falling. This could produce degenerate chain reactions or emergent combo systems. v0.3 should prototype and rule on whether cascade + nesting produces acceptable gameplay or is relegated to an arcade-only mode.

### 18.2 Multiplayer garbage (v0.3 design)
Proposed: opponents' nests send you low-density "hollow" garbage slabs — slabs that appear fully occupied but contain a single subcell aperture. These must be sealed (surrounded on 5+ faces) before they count as real occupancy for clearing purposes, forcing defensive nesting.

### 19.2 Channel axis computation
```python
def compute_channel_axis(voxels):
    bbx = max_x - min_x + 1
    bby = max_y - min_y + 1
    bbz = max_z - min_z + 1

    prismatic_axes = []
    for axis, extent in [('x', bbx), ('y', bby), ('z', bbz)]:
        if extent >= 2 and is_prismatic(voxels, axis):
            prismatic_axes.append((axis, extent))

    if prismatic_axes:
        # Max extent, tiebreak x > y > z
        return max(prismatic_axes, key=lambda a: (a[1], -ord(a[0])))[0]

    # Fallback: longest bounding-box extent
    extents = [('x', bbx), ('y', bby), ('z', bbz)]
    return max(extents, key=lambda a: (a[1], -ord(a[0])))[0]
```

### 19.3 Spawn offset precomputation
```python
def precompute_spawn(piece, orientation, size_class):
    scale = {HALF: 1, NORMAL: 2, DOUBLE: 4}[size_class]
    voxels = piece.orientations[orientation]  # at 1× scale
    bbx = (max_x - min_x + 1) * scale
    bby = (max_y - min_y + 1) * scale
    bbz = (max_z - min_z + 1) * scale

    if bbx > 16 or bby > 16:
        return UNSPAWNABLE

    sx = (16 - bbx) // 2
    sy = (16 - bby) // 2
    sz = 32 - bbz  # bottom of spawn buffer
    return (sx, sy, sz)
```
