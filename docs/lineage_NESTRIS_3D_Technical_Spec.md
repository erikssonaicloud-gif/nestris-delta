# NESTRIS 3D — Technical Specification

**Version 0.1 (Design Draft)**
**Genre:** 3D falling-block puzzle / spatial-density survival
**Document scope:** Piece geometry and generation math, coordinate systems, rotation and kick systems, collision, post-lock movement ("Seam Slide"), nesting mechanics, clearing, scoring, and difficulty. Rendering notes are included only where they constrain engineering.

---

## 1. Overview and Terminology

NESTRIS 3D is played in a vertical rectangular well. Pieces are **mated polycubes**: three-dimensional solids generated by a multiplicative (intersection-product) combination of two classic tetrominoes embedded in perpendicular planes. Pieces spawn in one of three size classes (½×, 1×, 2×). The player survives by **densifying** the stack — nesting small pieces into the concavities and pockets of larger ones — because 3D geometry wastes volume far faster than 2D Tetris does, and wasted volume is death.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Unit (u)** | The edge length of one cell of a normal-size (1×) piece. |
| **Subcell** | The engine's true grid resolution: ½ u. All geometry, collision, and clearing operate in subcells. |
| **Well** | The play volume. Default 8 u × 8 u × 16 u = **16 × 16 × 32 subcells** (W × D × H). |
| **Parent tetromino** | One of the 7 classic shapes {I, O, T, S, Z, L, J} as a planar 4-cell polyomino. |
| **Mated piece** | The 3D solid produced by the mating product of two parent tetrominoes (§3). |
| **Slab** | One horizontal layer of subcells (16 × 16 = 256 subcells). The clearing granularity. |
| **Pocket** | An empty region enclosed or shielded by a placed piece's geometry; the target volume for nesting (§9). |
| **Seam Slide** | The single-axis post-lock movement grace window (§8). |

---

## 2. Coordinate System, Units, and the Well

Right-handed coordinates. **x** = width (left/right), **y** = depth (toward/away from camera), **z** = height (up). Gravity acts along −z.

All positions and extents are stored as integer subcell coordinates. The well occupies `x ∈ [0,16)`, `y ∈ [0,16)`, `z ∈ [0,32)` in subcells. Two additional hidden slabs above the well (`z ∈ [32,36)`) form the **spawn buffer**; a piece resting with any voxel at `z ≥ 32` at lock time triggers the fail state (§14).

The choice of ½ u resolution means every size class lands on the integer grid:

| Size class | Scale | Cell edge in subcells | A cell's voxel count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Half | 0.5× | 1 | 1 subcell |
| Normal | 1× | 2 | 8 subcells (2×2×2) |
| Double | 2× | 4 | 64 subcells (4×4×4) |

There is deliberately no finer resolution and no rotation off the 90° lattice: every voxel of every piece at every legal orientation and position is axis-aligned and integer-addressed. This keeps collision exact and branch-free (§6).

## 3. Piece Geometry: The Mating Product

### 3.1 Parent embedding

Each parent tetromino is a set of 4 cells in a plane. For the mating operation:

- Parent **A** is embedded in the **XZ plane**: `A ⊂ ℤ²`, cells addressed `(x, z)`.
- Parent **B** is embedded in the **YZ plane**: `B ⊂ ℤ²`, cells addressed `(y, z)`.

The two planes share the **z axis** (the vertical). Each parent may be used in any of its planar orientations (up to 4 rotations and, for chiral pieces, mirror is *not* allowed — S and Z remain distinct, as do L and J).

### 3.2 The product operator ⊗

The mated piece **M = A ⊗ B** is defined multiplicatively on indicator functions. Writing `𝟙_A(x,z) ∈ {0,1}` and `𝟙_B(y,z) ∈ {0,1}`:

```
𝟙_M(x, y, z)  =  𝟙_A(x, z) · 𝟙_B(y, z)
```

A voxel exists at `(x,y,z)` **iff** its XZ shadow falls inside A **and** its YZ shadow falls inside B. Equivalently, per horizontal row: if row `z` of A contains the x-values `R_A(z)` and row `z` of B contains the y-values `R_B(z)`, then row `z` of M is the Cartesian product `R_A(z) × R_B(z)`.

**Volume formula.** With `r_A(z) = |R_A(z)|` and `r_B(z) = |R_B(z)|`:

```
V(M) = Σ_z  r_A(z) · r_B(z)
```

Volume ranges from 4 cells (two vertical I's) to 16 cells (two horizontal I's in the same row). This is the literal sense in which the geometry is *multiplicative*: each height-row of the solid is the arithmetic product of the two silhouettes' row widths.

### 3.3 Vertical alignment offset δ

Parents may occupy different z-extents (a vertical I spans 4 rows; a horizontal I spans 1). Before taking the product, B is shifted vertically by an integer offset **δ**, and the product is taken over the overlapping rows. Different δ values yield different — sometimes radically different — solids from the same parent pair. δ is therefore part of the piece's identity: a generated piece is fully specified by the tuple `(A, orient_A, B, orient_B, δ, size)`.

### 3.4 Validation rules (procedural generation)

The generator enumerates candidate tuples and accepts a piece only if all of the following hold:

1. **Non-degeneracy:** `V(M) ≥ 4` cells.
2. **Boundedness:** `V(M) ≤ 16` cells and the bounding box fits in 4×4×4 cells.
3. **Connectivity:** the voxel set is face-connected (6-connectivity). Disconnected products are rejected outright — no largest-component salvage, because a salvaged fragment no longer honestly reflects its parents.
4. **Spawnability:** at least one of the piece's 24 orientations fits within the well's horizontal cross-section at its size class. (A 2× piece with a 4-cell-wide footprint is 16 subcells wide — exactly the well width — and is legal but cannot be translated laterally in that orientation; the generator permits it, the difficulty curve controls its frequency.)
5. **Canonical deduplication:** two tuples producing congruent voxel sets (identical up to the 24 rotations) collapse to one canonical piece, so the bag isn't secretly biased toward high-symmetry shapes.

Enumeration over 7 parents × ≤4 orientations each × legal δ values, after deduplication, yields the **generated set** of roughly 60–90 distinct solids (exact count is an implementation output, not a design input; the bag system in §13 samples from it).

### 3.5 Worked examples

Using row notation `z: {x-values} ⊗ {y-values}`:

**T ⊗ T, both stem-down (the CRADLE).** A = T with stem at bottom: row 0 = {1}, row 1 = {0,1,2}. Same for B. Product: row 0 = 1×1 = 1 cell at (1,1,0); row 1 = 3×3 = 9 cells. **V = 10.** The result is a 3×3 plate levitating on a single central stem — leaving a ring of **8 empty cells of overhang** beneath the plate. This is the archetypal nesting host.

**L ⊗ T (the GNOMON).** A = L: row 0 = {0,1}, row 1 = {0}, row 2 = {0}. B = T stem-up: row 0 = {0,1,2}, row 1 = {1}. δ = 0 product: row 0 = 2×3 = 6 cells; row 1 = 1×1 = 1 cell at (0,1,1). **V = 7.** A 2×3 slab with one off-center stud.

**S ⊗ Z (the HELIX).** A = S: row 0 = {1,2}, row 1 = {0,1}. B = Z: row 0 = {0,1}, row 1 = {1,2}. Product: two 2×2 plates offset diagonally in opposite senses, joined through the shared (1,1) column. **V = 8.** A chiral step that creates a wraparound pocket when two HELIXes interlock.

**O ⊗ O (the REACTOR).** 2×2 on both silhouettes over 2 rows: **V = 8**, a perfect 2×2×2 cube. The only fully convex piece of volume 8; trivially stackable, contributes nothing to nesting.

**I ⊗ I, both vertical (the MONOLITH).** 1×1×4 column, **V = 4.** The minimum-volume piece and the game's gap-filling scalpel, especially at ½ size.

### 3.6 Legendary pieces

Legendaries are hand-curated tuples with fixed names, guaranteed bag appearances (§13), and distinct emissive colorways. They are mechanically identical to generated pieces — no special rules — but are chosen because their geometry anchors strategy:

| Name | Tuple | V | Strategic identity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MONOLITH** | I(vert) ⊗ I(vert) | 4 | Precision gap filler |
| **REACTOR** | O ⊗ O | 8 | Convex foundation block |
| **CRADLE** | T(stem-down) ⊗ T(stem-down) | 10 | Premier nesting host: 8-cell pocket ring |
| **HELIX** | S ⊗ Z | 8 | Chiral interlock; pairs with its mirror |
| **PLATE** | I(horiz) ⊗ I(horiz), δ=0 | 16 | Maximum volume; slab finisher and pocket "lid" |

## 4. Size Classes

Every drawn piece is instantiated at one of three scales. Scale multiplies the piece's cell lattice, not just its render size:

| Class | Cell edge | Typical bounding box (subcells) | Bag weight (baseline) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Half (½×) | 1 subcell | ≤ 4×4×4 | 25% |
| Normal (1×) | 2 subcells | ≤ 8×8×8 | 55% |
| Double (2×) | 4 subcells | ≤ 16×16×16 | 20% |

Three consequences the engine must honor:

1. **Sub-lattice placement.** A ½× piece can occupy odd subcell coordinates — it addresses the grid at twice the spatial frequency of a 1× piece. This is what makes nesting *geometrically real*: a ½× MONOLITH (4×1×1 subcells) slides into seams no 1× piece can enter.
2. **Fall speed scales inversely with size.** Perceptually, larger masses should feel heavier but fall no faster; mechanically, Half pieces fall at 1.25× the current gravity rate, Doubles at 0.8×, giving the player more decision time on exactly the pieces that can ruin a board.
3. **Doubles create the pockets that Halves fill.** A 2× CRADLE's pocket ring is 8 cells × 64 subcells = 512 subcells of shielded volume — enough to swallow several entire ½× pieces. The size system and the nesting system are one system.

## 5. Orientation and Rotation

### 5.1 Orientation space

Pieces rotate on the 90° lattice only. The orientation space is the **chiral octahedral rotation group** (order 24). Each piece precomputes up to 24 voxel sets at load time (fewer after symmetry collapse — the REACTOR has 1 distinct orientation, the MONOLITH 3). Runtime rotation is a table lookup, never a matrix transform.

Inputs map to quarter-turn generators: **pitch** (about x), **yaw** (about z), **roll** (about y). Orientation state is an index 0–23; each generator is a precomputed permutation of indices.

### 5.2 3D kick table

When a rotation is blocked, the engine attempts translated placements in strict order, at offsets scaled to the piece's size class (offsets below in subcells for a 1× piece; halve for ½×, double for 2×):

```
(0,0,0), (−2,0,0), (+2,0,0), (0,−2,0), (0,+2,0),
(0,0,+2), (−2,−2,0), (+2,+2,0), (−2,+2,0), (+2,−2,0), (0,0,+4)
```

The first collision-free candidate is accepted; if none succeed, the rotation is refused and orientation is unchanged. The two upward kicks (`+z`) permit T-spin-style tuck maneuvers under overhangs — with CRADLE-class pockets in play, upward kicks are the primary skilled-play verb, so their position late in the table (making them a last resort rather than an accident) is intentional.

### 5.3 Rotation legality inside pockets

No special-case rules: a rotation inside a pocket is legal iff the destination voxel set is collision-free after kicks. Pocket ceilings naturally forbid most rotations, which is what makes pre-rotation *before* entering a pocket a skill.

## 6. Collision and Occupancy

### 6.1 Data structure

The well is a bitboard: one bit per subcell, organized as 32 slabs × 4 × `uint64` (256 bits per slab), 128 words total. The active piece is stored the same way, as a sparse list of occupied slab-words at its current position.

### 6.2 Tests

- **Broad phase:** integer AABB of the piece vs. well bounds; reject out-of-bounds immediately.
- **Narrow phase:** for each slab the piece intersects, `piece_bits & well_bits ≠ 0` ⇒ collision. Worst case (2× piece, 16-slab extent) is 64 AND operations.

All movement (gravity step, lateral step, rotation candidate, kick candidate, Seam Slide step) reduces to this one test at a candidate position. There is no continuous physics, no floating point, no tunneling.

### 6.3 Lock condition

A piece **locks** when a gravity step is blocked and the **lock delay** (500 ms baseline, −15 ms/level, floor 200 ms) elapses without a successful downward move. Successful lateral moves or rotations during lock delay reset it, up to a cap of 8 resets (anti-infinite-stall, per modern Tetris convention). On lock, the piece's bits are OR-ed into the well and the Seam Slide window opens (§8).

---

## 7. Gravity and Fall Timing

Gravity is expressed in subcells per second and follows a per-level curve:

```
g(level) = 2 · 1.28^(level − 1)   subcells/sec      (levels 1–20, then fixed)
```

modified by size class (×1.25 Half, ×1.0 Normal, ×0.8 Double). Soft drop multiplies current gravity ×20; hard drop is instantaneous with lock delay skipped but the **Seam Slide window preserved** — hard drop commits your column, not your final seat.

Level advances every 10 slabs cleared.

## 8. Seam Slide (Post-Lock Grace Movement)

The mechanic formalizing "movement after placement is possible for a short time on a restricted axis within the physical constraints of the piece's geometry."

### 8.1 The channel axis

Every piece has exactly one **channel axis**, computed once at generation time from its geometry:

1. If the piece is **prismatic** along some axis *a* — every cross-section perpendicular to *a* is identical — then *a* is the channel axis. (The MONOLITH is prismatic along its long axis; the PLATE along both horizontal axes, resolved by rule 3.)
2. Otherwise the channel axis is the axis of the **longest bounding-box extent**, the direction in which the solid most resembles a slideable prism.
3. Ties break in fixed priority **x > y > z**.

The channel axis rotates with the piece: it is stored per-orientation in the precomputed tables. A piece whose channel axis currently points along z can Seam Slide only vertically — usually useless, occasionally a lifesaver under an overhang — which makes *final orientation* a resource, not just a fit decision.

### 8.2 The window

On lock, a window of `W(level) = max(120, 400 − 10·level)` ms opens. During it:

- The piece may translate in **±1-subcell steps along its channel axis only**. No rotation. No other axis. Each step requires a collision-free destination.
- Each successful step costs 40 ms of remaining window (sliding is not free).
- The window ends at expiry or on the player's confirm input, whichever is first.

### 8.3 Settle rule

If Seam Sliding leaves the piece unsupported (no voxel has an occupied subcell or the floor directly beneath it), then at window close the piece **drops straight down** to the first supported position and hard-locks with no further grace. You can slide off a shelf; the floor will collect you.

### 8.4 Why it exists

Seam Slide is the delivery mechanism for nesting. A ½× piece dropped down an open shaft can, in its grace window, slide *laterally into a pocket* whose ceiling made direct vertical entry impossible. Without Seam Slide, CRADLE-ring pockets under existing overhangs would be mostly unreachable and the density economy (§11) collapses.

## 9. Nesting and Pockets

### 9.1 Pocket detection (host-side)

When a piece locks, the engine computes its **pocket field** within the piece's bounding box inflated by 1 subcell:

An empty subcell belongs to the pocket field iff, along **at least 4 of its 6 axis directions**, a ray hits one of the host's voxels before exiting the inflated box. Pocket cells are grouped into connected **pockets** by flood fill; pockets smaller than 4 subcells are discarded as noise. Each pocket stores its volume and its **aperture** (the set of its cells with an unshielded direction — the mouth you enter through).

Pocket fields are recomputed lazily for hosts adjacent to any newly locked piece, since a neighbor can seal or subdivide a pocket.

### 9.2 Nest registration (guest-side)

When a piece locks, the engine checks its voxels against the pocket fields of all hosts whose inflated boxes it intersects. If **≥ 75% of the guest's voxels lie inside pockets of a single host**, a **nest** is registered: guest → host. Nests are events (they fire scoring and the density system), not ongoing state — the voxels simply merge into the well like any others.

### 9.3 Nesting and clearing

Nested voxels participate in slab clears exactly like all voxels. There is no separate "clear the nest" rule; the reward for nesting is (a) the immediate nest bonus (§12), (b) the density contribution (§11), and (c) the plain geometric fact that a filled pocket is a fillable slab. The design principle: **nesting is never a special case in the clearing rules — it is a strictly geometric act with economic consequences.**

## 10. Slab Clears

A slab clears when all 256 of its subcells are occupied. On clear:

1. All voxels in the slab are deleted.
2. Everything above descends by whole slabs (**sticky column gravity**, classic Tetris behavior: the well contents above translate down 1 subcell per cleared slab, as a rigid mass per column). A **Cascade** variant (connected-component re-fall) is specced as a selectable mode but is *not* the default; cascade physics plus nesting produces chain economies that belong in a variant, not the core game.
3. Simultaneous multi-slab clears score super-linearly (§12).

Because a 1× piece is 2 subcells tall, the natural clearing rhythm is 2-slab pairs; single-slab clears are the signature of successful ½×-piece surgery, and 4-slab ("QUAD") clears require deliberate Double-piece foundations.

## 11. Pressure and the Density Economy

The stack's **density** is measured over its occupied envelope:

```
density = (occupied subcells) / (subcells in all slabs at or below the highest occupied slab)
```

Density is the survival currency and closes the loop on the game's premise — 3D stacking wastes volume unless you nest:

- **density ≥ 0.80:** gravity multiplier ×0.9 (the stack "breathes"; visual: floor grid pulses cool cyan).
- **0.5 < density < 0.80:** neutral.
- **density ≤ 0.50:** gravity multiplier ×1.15 and the **Pressure** vignette appears (rising magenta bloom at the well rim).

The multipliers apply on top of the level curve. A player who stacks 3D pieces the way one stacks 2D tetrominoes will hover near 0.45 density and be accelerated to death; a player who nests will hold 0.75+ and buy time. This is the enforcement mechanism for the core strategy statement: *lasting longest requires nesting the small pieces inside the larger ones.*

## 12. Scoring

| Event | Score |
|---|---|
| Soft drop | 1 / subcell |
| Hard drop | 2 / subcell |
| Single slab clear | 100 × level |
| Double slab | 300 × level |
| Triple slab | 500 × level |
| QUAD (4 slabs) | 800 × level |
| 5+ slabs ("COLLAPSE") | 1200 × level + 200/extra slab |
| **Nest bonus** | 4 × (nested subcells) × level |
| **Perfect nest** (100% of guest voxels pocketed AND pocket ≥ 90% filled) | ×3 the nest bonus |
| Seam Slide save (nest registered on a piece that Seam Slid ≥ 2 steps) | +150 × level |
| All-clear (empty well) | 3000 × level |

Back-to-back multi-slab clears carry a ×1.5 chain multiplier, Tetris-standard.

## 13. Randomizer

A **dual-bag** system:

- **Shape bag:** the generated set (§3.4) is partitioned into difficulty tiers by convexity deficiency (V ÷ bounding-box volume). Each bag of 12 draws: 5 from the convex tier, 4 from the moderate tier, 2 from the pathological tier, plus **exactly 1 legendary**, shuffled.
- **Size bag:** an independent 20-draw bag containing 5 Half, 11 Normal, 4 Double, shuffled; sizes are assigned to shapes at spawn.

Preview queue shows the next 4 pieces *with sizes*, rendered at true relative scale. One hold slot, Tetris-standard rules (no re-hold until a spawn).

The independence of the two bags is a deliberate strategic constraint: you can see a ½× MONOLITH coming, but you cannot make one come.

---

## 14. Fail State

The game ends when either (a) a spawning piece's initial position collides with the well contents, or (b) a piece locks with any voxel in the spawn buffer (`z ≥ 32`). Death animation: the neon edge-glow of every placed piece gutters out slab by slab from the top down — the smothering, rendered.

---

## 15. Visual Language (Engineering-Relevant Notes)

Sleek minimalist neon; the aesthetic doubles as information design, so these are functional requirements:

- **Material:** pieces render as near-black glass volumes with **emissive edges** (bloom-post-processed). Fill opacity ~12% so that pockets and nested guests remain visible *through* hosts — occlusion transparency is not decoration, it is how the player reads nest opportunities.
- **Color channels:** hue encodes parent-pair family (generated pieces), fixed signature hues for legendaries; **edge intensity encodes size class** (Half = thin crisp lines, Double = heavy corona). Nested guests re-tint to their host's hue at 60% saturation on registration, visually "absorbed."
- **Ghost systems:** standard drop-ghost at the landing position, plus a **pocket ghost** — when the active piece's current orientation could register a nest reachable via the remaining Seam Slide budget, the target pocket's aperture edges pulse. This is computed with the same pocket-field data from §9.1; no additional geometry pass.
- **Well:** wireframe grid at subcell pitch on the floor, unit pitch on the walls, so the two resolutions of the game are always both on screen.

## 16. Appendix: Core Pseudocode

### 16.1 Piece generation

```
for A in TETROMINOES, oA in orientations(A):
  for B in TETROMINOES, oB in orientations(B):
    for δ in legal_offsets(A@oA, B@oB):
      M = {}
      for z in overlap_rows(A@oA, B@oB, δ):
        for x in row(A@oA, z):
          for y in row(B@oB, z − δ):
            M.add((x, y, z))
      if 4 ≤ |M| ≤ 16 and face_connected(M) and fits_4cube(M):
        register(canonicalize_under_24_rotations(M), tuple=(A,oA,B,oB,δ))
```

### 16.2 Pocket field (host H, inflated AABB Ω)

```
for each empty subcell c in Ω:
  shielded = 0
  for dir in ±x, ±y, ±z:
    walk from c along dir until leaving Ω or hitting a voxel of H
    if hit: shielded += 1
  if shielded ≥ 4: mark c as pocket cell
pockets = flood_fill(pocket cells); discard pockets with volume < 4
```

### 16.3 Nest check on lock (guest G)

```
for each host H with inflated_AABB(H) ∩ AABB(G) ≠ ∅:
  inside = |voxels(G) ∩ pocket_field(H)|
  if inside / |voxels(G)| ≥ 0.75:
    register_nest(G → H)          // fires scoring + density event
    break                          // one host per nest
```
